When an urge or hard feeling hits

Pause. Connect. Choose.

You are more than a difficult moment. Shame and isolation can make recovery harder; reach for support early and keep your plan simple.

  1. Pause: put the device down, leave the setting, breathe slowly, and name what you're feeling.
  2. Connect: contact your companion or another trusted person in the support plan you've agreed on.
  3. Choose: do the next safe, mission-appropriate thing—walk, pray, drink water, or return to service.

Mission life

Keep the plan grounded

Let the people and rhythms around you help you stay steady.

  • Shape exercise, food, device use, and personal study around your mission rules and health needs.
  • Make study adaptable: a chapter, a few verses, audio, or a verse revisited with a question.
  • Build companionship into the plan: check in, serve together, and ask for help before a struggle grows.
  • Review the day with curiosity, not condemnation. Adjust one thing for tomorrow.
For the days that feel heavy

Faith and help can go together

ADHD, depression, and anxiety deserve compassionate care. Prayer and scripture can be meaningful supports alongside a licensed mental-health professional and any treatment plan. If you can, tell a trusted family member, mission leader, or clinician what support would help.

If you may hurt yourself or someone else, contact local emergency services or a crisis line now and tell a trusted person nearby. In the U.S. or Canada, call or text 988.
